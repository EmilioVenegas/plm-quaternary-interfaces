## 3. Track A — PTM-site chemistry-vs-conservation confound

### 3.1 The family-granularity check, done properly (and it changes the design)

The brief assumed "phospho-acceptor {S,T,Y} has 3 members, glycosylation Asn {N,D,Q} has
3 members — better than metal thiolate's 1-member {C} problem." **Both assumptions are
chemically wrong**, and the correction is the single most important design finding here.

| Locus | Brief assumed | Chemically correct family | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phospho Ser/Thr site | {S,T,Y}, 3 members | **{S,T}**, 2 members | 1 preserving substitution per position |
| Phospho **Tyr** site | {S,T,Y}, 3 members | **{Y}**, 1 member | **zero** preserving substitutions — Tyr kinases are a separate enzyme class from Ser/Thr kinases; S/T at a Tyr site is not phospho-preserving |
| Glyc sequon **Asn** (+0) | {N,D,Q}, 3 members | **{N}**, 1 member | **zero** preserving substitutions — no other residue is a glycosyl acceptor. N→D and N→Q both *abolish* glycosylation; they are steric/charge mimics, not chemical ones |
| Glyc sequon **+2** (the S/T) | not considered | **{S,T,C}**, 3 members | 2 preserving substitutions per position — **the only locus in the glycosylation track where a preserving substitution exists at all** |

Measured confirmation of the collapse, from real data: at the 117 sequon-Asn positions
with DMS coverage there are 2,145 available substitutions and **0** of them are
modification-preserving. That is the CYP2C9/GAL4 1-member-family failure, reproduced
exactly as the brief feared — but it hits the *Asn acceptor*, not the loci the brief
worried about.

The `{S,T,C}` family at +2 is **primary-source verified in-session** (I read the paper,
not a summary). Bause & Legler 1981, *Biochem J* 195:639–644, PMID 7316978,
`10.1042/bj1950639`, verbatim from the abstract:

> "the threonine-, serine- and cysteine-containing derivatives could be glycosylated,
> although at very different rates, whereas the valine and O-methylthreonine analogues
> did not work as glycosyl acceptors. Replacement of threonine by serine resulted in a
> 4-fold decrease in Vmax, and about a 10-fold increase in Km for glycosyl transfer.
> Replacement of serine by cysteine again decreased acceptor activity 2-3-fold."

This gives the track a graded three-level preserving scale at +2 (Thr > Ser > Cys ≫
everything else) rather than a binary contrast — strictly more statistical structure to
test against. It also means a delegated agent's claim that Cys at +2 abolishes
glycosylation was **false**; see §7.2.

### 3.4 Matched-control decision (brief task A5): **within-assay**, not an external protein

**Recommendation: non-PTM Ser/Thr positions inside the same assays.** Reasoning:

- It removes the weakness of the metal design. BLAT_ECOLX as an external control
  introduced a second protein-family confound into a test that was already about
  position class; the null was directionally consistent with the control, which is
  exactly what you cannot interpret when protein identity and site class covary.
- It can be made **residue-identity matched**, which an external control cannot: control
  positions are wild-type Ser/Thr carrying the *same* `{S,T,C}` preserving substitution
  as the sequon +2 sites. Same assay, same selection, same residue, same available
  chemistry — **only PTM status differs**. This neutralises the family-size confound that
  destroyed two metal candidates.
- The "is it really non-functional?" objection is handled by matching on conservation
  rather than asserting neutrality: stratify control positions against PTM positions on
  ESM2's masked wild-type log-probability (and/or ProteinGym MSA column entropy). Since
  the hypothesis *is* "the model treats PTM sites as generically conserved", matching on
  the model's own conservation estimate is the correct isolation of chemistry from
  conservation, not circularity.
- It is not power-limited: **599 control positions, n = 11,283**, measured.

Keep an external non-PTM protein only as a secondary robustness arm, not the primary
comparison.

### 3.5 Worked numeric examples (brief acceptance criterion: ≥1 per track)

**Example A1 — ERK2/MAPK1 activation-loop Thr185 (`MK01_HUMAN_Brenan_2016`).**
Structural validation: `target_seq[182:190]` = `FLTEYVAT`, i.e. the canonical **TEY**
motif with T185/Y187 exactly where human UniProt numbering says (literature often uses
rat T183/Y185 — the offset was checked, not assumed).

| | ESM2 log-odds | measured DMS |
|---|---|---|
| T185**S** (preserving) | **−3.34** (rank 1 of 19, most tolerated by a 6-nat margin) | **−10.43** (rank 19 of 19, *worst*) |
| mean of 18 abolishing | −11.36 | −9.15 |
| **gap (preserving − abolishing)** | **+8.02** | **−1.28** |

ESM2 gives the chemically conservative substitution an enormous pass; the experiment
says it is the single worst substitution at that position. **The sign is inverted.** This
is precisely the confound Track A hypothesises, in a fully characterised regulatory
site — and it is the kind of effect that a pooled all-positions Spearman correlation
cannot see.

**Example A2 — SARS-CoV-2 Spike N343 glycan (`SPIKE_SARS2_Starr_2020_binding`).**
Structural validation: `target_seq[340:347]` = `VFNATRF`, i.e. sequon N343-A344-T345.

- At the **+2 locus T345**: ESM2 ranks T345S first (−0.41 vs −2.05 mean abolishing, gap
  +1.64); DMS agrees (−0.02 vs −0.29, gap +0.27). Model correct here.
- At the **Asn acceptor N343**: all 19 substitutions are abolishing. Preserving
  substitutions available: **0**. The 1-member-family collapse, shown numerically.

Two sites, opposite outcomes — which is the argument for running the test rather than
assuming its result.

### 3.6 Floor-censoring check — the artifact that hollowed out Track B does not hit Track A

The GFP Cys-pair data collapsed not because it was absent but because it was *censored*:
11 of 13 variants pinned at one repeated value. Since the same failure would quietly

was accepted. Artifact: `analysis/results/trackA_censoring_diagnostic.csv`.

One real exception: **`ENV_HV1BR_Haddox_2016` [glyc+2]** — 570 substitutions with

## 4. Track B — disulfide-bond paired epistasis

### 4.1 The scoring mechanics, derived and validated (brief task B5)

Both a joint (double-mask) and a conditional (autoregressive-path) score are obtainable;
neither needs more than one forward pass per term. Implemented in
`analysis/recon_ptm_disulfide.py::score_pair`:

$$\text{additive} = \sum_{k\in\{i,j\}} \Big[\log P(x_k^{mt}\mid x_{\setminus k}) - \log P(x_k^{wt}\mid x_{\setminus k})\Big]$$
$$\text{joint} = \sum_{k\in\{i,j\}} \Big[\log P(x_k^{mt}\mid x_{\setminus \{i,j\}}) - \log P(x_k^{wt}\mid x_{\setminus \{i,j\}})\Big]$$
$$\text{conditional} = \tfrac{1}{2}\big[P_{i\to j} + P_{j\to i}\big],\quad P_{i\to j} = \Big[\log\tfrac{P(m_i\mid x_{\setminus i})}{P(w_i\mid x_{\setminus i})}\Big] + \Big[\log\tfrac{P(m_j\mid x_{i:=m_i},\,\setminus j)}{P(w_j\mid x_{i:=m_i},\,\setminus j)}\Big]$$
$$\varepsilon = \text{conditional} - \text{additive}$$

Masking both positions in **one** pass gives each position's conditional with *both*
hidden — that is the joint quantity, not two independent marginals. So no, two passes
are not required for the joint term; they are required for the conditional path, and the
path must be symmetrized because $P_{i\to j} \neq P_{j\to i}$.

**Validation, mirroring the VIM-2 3H/DCH discipline** — a real disulfide plus two
negative controls, structural ground truth from real `SSBOND` records:

| pair | structural status (real PDB record) | additive | joint | conditional | **ε (joint−add)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TEM-1 C75/C121 → A,A | `1BTL`: `SSBOND 1 CYS A 77 CYS A 123` | −6.83 | −2.43 | −4.08 | **+4.41** |
| TEM-1 C75/C121 → S,S | same bond | −7.48 | −3.21 | −4.54 | **+4.26** |
| GFP C48/C70 → A,A | `1EMA`: **0 SSBOND records** — free thiols | +4.51 | +4.85 | +4.76 | +0.34 |
| TEM-1 G76/S122 → A,A | non-Cys, identical sequence separation | +2.15 | +2.15 | +2.19 | +0.003 |

**The mechanism check passes and the result is scientifically interesting:** ε is ~13×
larger at a real disulfide than at a chemically similar free-thiol Cys pair, and ~1,300×
larger than at a distance-matched non-Cys pair. The sign is **positive** — the joint
score is *less* deleterious than the additive sum, which is the biophysically correct
direction (once one partner is gone, removing the second no longer leaves an unpaired
reactive thiol).

Directionally corroborated by measured data: in `BLAT_ECOLX_Stiffler_2015`, **C75A =
−0.125** against an assay mean of −1.256 — nearly neutral — while every non-Ala/Ser
substitution at 75 and 121 sits at −2 to −3.3. The TEM-1 disulfide is dispensable, as
ESM2's positive ε implies.

**So ESM2 is not blind to disulfide coupling. The additive *protocol* discards it.**
That reframes the hypothesis from a representational deficiency to a protocol artifact —
a weaker and less novel claim than the brief assumed.

### 4.4 MaveDB: the last escape hatch, closed

A null from two repositories is not a proof about the world, so the largest DMS
repository was swept as well. MaveDB exposes no unfiltered list endpoint and its text
search caps at 100 hits, so a candidate pool was built from a 35-term sweep biased hard
toward disulfide-bearing protein classes (disulfide, cysteine, lysozyme, BPTI,
ribonuclease, insulin, scFv, antibody, nanobody, VHH, immunoglobulin, EGF, toxin,
defensin, protease inhibitor, thioredoxin, secreted, glycoprotein, spike, envelope,
hemagglutinin…) *and* toward the assay designs that produce multi-mutants at all
(epistasis, double mutant, combinatorial, folding, stability, thermodynamic).

Detection is on the HGVS protein string itself — `p.[Cys47Arg;Cys69Arg]` — so the
wild-type residue comes from the record rather than from a translation or alignment that
could be wrong.

| | |
|---|---|
| Score sets in candidate pool (all with target sequence) | **494** of 2,805 in MaveDB |
| Score sets containing **any** multi-variant | **143** |
| Multi-variant rows scanned | **814,077** |
| Rows substituting **2+ wild-type cysteines** | **26** |
| Score sets containing such rows | **2** |

The two hits are `urn:mavedb:00000080-a-1` and `-a-2` — the nucleotide and amino-acid
score sets of **one** dataset, PubMed **27193686**, Sarkisyan et al. 2016 "Local fitness
landscape of the green fluorescent protein". That is the *same* avGFP experiment
ProteinGym ships as `GFP_AEQVI_Sarkisyan_2016`, at the same pair (MaveDB 47/69 vs
ProteinGym 48/70 — a one-residue Met-numbering offset), the same 13 variants, and the
same pair that `1EMA` shows carries **no disulfide**.

Note the strongest single negative: GB1 (`urn:mavedb:00000105-a-1`, Olson 2014's
exhaustive 4-position combinatorial library) contributes **516,423** multi-variant rows
and **zero** Cys–Cys pairs, because GB1 is cysteine-free. The most systematically
combinatorial DMS dataset in existence cannot contribute a single observation.

Every one of the 494 score sets resolved (transient HTTP 502s are retried with backoff
and any residual failure is reported as `FAILED` rather than counted as a null — a
silently dropped score set would manufacture exactly the answer we want, so it is not
permitted to).

**Grand total across all three repositories: 2,793,651 multi-mutant rows examined; the
only Cys–Cys observations anywhere are 13 floor-censored, multiply-substituted variants
at a pair of free thiols in GFP.**

### 4.5 Substitute designs, and Track B's residual (weaker) opportunity

Classic disulfide-engineering systems were checked individually (T4 lysozyme, BPTI,
RNase A, HEWL, insulin, scFv). They fail for one shared structural reason: **the field
mutates cysteines in pairs on purpose**, to avoid unpaired-thiol scrambling and
aggregation. RNase A's Raines-lab series measures 4 paired double mutants and **zero**
single-Cys variants; BPTI's single-Cys variants were deliberately omitted. A 4-state
double-mutant cycle needs both singles — so the very artifact-avoidance that makes those
experiments clean makes them unusable here. (This section is delegated-agent research;
individual citations are **not** independently verified by me — see §7.2.)

What Track B *could* still support: a **model-internal** characterisation — "ε is
systematically larger at real disulfides than at conservation-matched control pairs" —
using UniProt/PDB disulfide annotations with no experimental double mutants at all. n
would be large (191 verified bonds in ProteinGym alone, effectively all of the PDB if
wanted), the ε signal is already demonstrated at 4.41 vs 0.003 nats, and §4.1 is a
complete, validated method. But it can only claim the model is *non-additive*, never that
it is *right*, and the novelty ground is now occupied (§5). That is a workshop note, not
the paper the brief was scoping.

## 7. Resource findings and honesty notes

### 7.1 The 8GB VRAM ceiling is not the binding constraint — four models are feasible

The brief called 8GB inference-only "the one hard, real constraint" and asked that larger
checkpoints be *checked* rather than assumed impossible. Checked. It is not the binding
constraint for this workload, because masked-marginal scoring needs roughly **one forward
pass per masked position — about 1,500 for all of Track A**, not millions. That makes
seconds-per-forward acceptable, and it makes system RAM substitutable for VRAM.

All measured on the RTX 4060 Laptop (8,188 MiB VRAM, 62 GB system RAM):

| model | params | mode | peak VRAM | forward @286 / @2000 | Track A (~1,500 positions) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `esm2_t33_650M_UR50D` | 0.65 B | GPU (fp32 in pipeline / fp16 here) | 2.68 / **1.45 GB** | 0.031 / 0.200 s | ~1 min |
| `esm2_t36_3B_UR50D` | 2.84 B | GPU fp16 | **5.93 GB** | 0.093 / 0.671 s | ~3 min |
| `biohub/ESMC-600M` | 0.58 B | GPU fp16 | **2.39 GB** | 0.022 / 0.147 s | <1 min |
| `biohub/ESMC-6B` | 6.35 B | GPU fp16 | — | **OOM** | infeasible |
| `biohub/ESMC-6B` | 6.35 B | **CPU offload** fp16, batch 128 | **2.93 GB** | 0.23 s / position | **~6 min** |

**ESM2-3B**: fp16, batch 1, reproduces the disulfide sanity check (argmax `C` at both
TEM-1 cysteines). Only 7 of 217 assays exceed 1,300 residues. Loading frictions worth
recording: the checkpoint ships `.bin` shards only and transformers 5 refuses
`torch.load` under torch 2.5.1, so weights must be loaded manually from **both** shards
(loading one silently yields 0.35 B parameters).

**ESMC-6B**: naive fp16-to-GPU OOMs, as 25.4 GB of fp32 weights (≈12.7 GB fp16) against
7.65 GB usable should. It runs via accelerate `cpu_offload`: weights sit in system RAM
and each submodule streams to the GPU on demand, so **peak VRAM is 0.16 GB at batch 1**.
ESMC's loader is not a transformers `PreTrainedModel` (it is EvolutionaryScale's own
`HubPreTrainedModel(nn.Module)`) and rejects `device_map`, but `cpu_offload` hooks any
`nn.Module`. Cost is PCIe-bandwidth-bound, so batching independent masked variants
amortises one weight stream across many positions — a **7.7× speedup**, 1.77 → 0.23 s
per position from batch 1 → 128, at 2.93 GB peak with headroom left.

Licence: MIT, Chan Zuckerberg Biohub, ungated (`LICENSE.md` read directly; the ESM org
has rebranded from EvolutionaryScale, and its flagship is now ESMC/ESMFold2).

Numerical caveats for a publication run: without `transformer-engine`/`xformers`/
`flash-attn`, ESMC falls back to pure-PyTorch LayerNorm/attention/RoPE and warns of
drift on the unnormalized residual stream — which it states shrinks to a few ULP after
the final LayerNorm. Our quantities are logit-space, so this is rounding noise, but the
fused kernels should be installed and a few positions spot-checked before publishing.
fp16 applies no quantisation, only fp16 rounding, which is immaterial at log-odds gaps of
1–8 nats.

**ESM2-15B** would need ~30 GB in fp16; feasible in principle by the same offload route
given 62 GB RAM, but untested here.

### 7.1b Model choice, and why ESM3 is excluded on data rather than hardware

ESM2-650M is the primary model for continuity: the metal-coordination null was obtained
with this exact model and protocol, so changing checkpoints would confound "different
result" with "different model". Masked-marginal scoring is also defined for ESM2/ESM-1v
(Meier et al.) and is what ProteinGym's baselines and the Kolchina/Kalinina benchmark use
— Track A's hypothesis is a claim about that protocol as practised.

But a single-checkpoint result invites "is this an ESM2 artifact?", and the hypothesis is
about the *protocol*, not one model. So the confirmatory run should replicate across
ESM2-650M, ESM2-3B, ESMC-600M and ESMC-6B — all four now measured feasible, total added
compute on the order of 15 minutes. An early cross-family signal already exists. Masked
WT log-probability at the two TEM-1 disulfide cysteines (target_seq 75 / 121), all four
models fp16 in one harness run, **every one recovering `C` as argmax**:

| model | logP(WT) @75 | logP(WT) @121 | C→A log-odds @121 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ESM2-650M | −0.096 | −0.048 | — |
| ESM2-3B | −0.001 | −0.000 | — |
| ESMC-600M | −0.055 | −0.001 | −7.80 |
| ESMC-6B | −0.148 | −0.004 | −8.36 |

Confidence at buried disulfide cysteines is therefore not a checkpoint quirk, and it
*increases* with ESM2 scale — which is exactly why the Track A contrast has to be run
across models rather than argued from one.

**ESM3 is deliberately excluded, and not for hardware reasons.** ESM3-open's training set
was filtered for biosecurity: Hayes et al. (*Science* 2025; preprint
`10.1101/2024.07.01.600583`) describe a **Viral Denylist of ~4 million UniProt sequences
annotated as viral**, plus ~147k non-viral and ~40k additional viral Select Agent
sequences, removed from training, with viral/toxin prompting stripped from the open
model. Track A's glycosylation arm is **110 of its 150 usable positions** and consists of
HIV-1 Env (3 strains), influenza HA (2), SARS-CoV-2 Spike and Zika E — every one viral
except ACE2. Scoring those sequons with ESM3 would make the nuisance variable identical
to the independent variable: a poor score could not be attributed to a PTM-chemistry
blind spot rather than to deletion of the relevant sequences from pretraining. Published
reports of reduced viral-fitness-prediction performance in the open model corroborate the
concern. ESM3 remains usable for the phospho arm (human kinases, TP53, PTEN) if a
multimodal comparison is ever wanted.

### 7.2 A delegated-agent claim was fabricated — treat unverified tags accordingly

A research subagent supplied this as a direct quote from "Bause, E. (*Biochem J* 1983,
209:331–336, PMID 6307300)":

> "The presence of a hydroxy amino acid (serine or threonine) in the tripeptide sequon
> Asn-X-Ser/Thr is a necessary and absolute prerequisite for N-glycosylation. Peptides in
> which the hydroxy amino acid is replaced by alanine, **cysteine**, or valine are not
> glycosylated."

I opened PMC1154098. That paper is "Structural requirements of N-glycosylation of
proteins. Studies with proline peptides as conformational probes", its PMID is **6847620**
(not 6307300), and it contains no such sentence. The hydroxy-amino-acid result belongs to
**Bause & Legler 1981** — which reports the **opposite** of the quoted claim: cysteine
peptides *are* glycosylated (§3.1). The quote, the attribution and the PMID were all
wrong, and the error pointed the design in the wrong direction.

Consequences applied here: every literature claim above is tagged
`[VERIFIED-IN-SESSION]` or `[SCOUT-REPORTED, not independently verified]`. The four
load-bearing claims — the two Track B novelty threats, the sequon chemistry, and the
disulfide structural ground truth — were all verified by me directly. Anything still
carrying a `[SCOUT-REPORTED]` tag must be re-read before it enters a manuscript.

### 7.3 What would overturn these verdicts

- **Track A → kill** if its usable positions were dominated by assay-floor censoring, the
  way the GFP Cys pairs were. **This was checked, not deferred (§3.6): 18 of 19
  assay × locus strata sit at 0.000 floor fraction, and dropping the one censored assay
  still leaves n = 12,173 / limiting cell 200.** No remaining kill condition of this
  kind. The live risks are now downstream ones a confirmatory run must handle: assay
  directionality sign conventions per DMS, and whether conservation matching survives
  using ESM2's own WT log-probability as the matching variable.
- **Track B → revive** only if a dataset appears with quantitative single *and* double
  Cys-removal measurements at a native disulfide. This is now a searched-and-closed
  negative, not an assumption: ProteinGym (1,769,456 multi-mutant rows), MegaScale
  (210,118 doubles) and MaveDB (814,077 multi-variant rows across 494 score sets) all
  return nothing, and the classic disulfide-engineering literature omits single-Cys
  variants by design. The cause is physical — an unpaired thiol scrambles and
  aggregates — so new data would have to come from an experiment nobody has a reason to
  run. Treat revival as requiring a *new wet-lab dataset*, not a better search.

### 7.4 A silent annotation failure nearly produced a false negative

The first pass reported PTEN as contributing **zero** usable positions and counted it
among the "named candidates that collapse". That was wrong, and the way it was wrong is
worth recording because it is the failure mode this whole reconnaissance exists to catch.

ProteinGym lists PTEN's `UniProt_ID` as `PTEN_HUMAN`. Querying UniProt by that entry name
returns accession **O00633** — a record with an **empty sequence and zero features** —
not human PTEN **P60484**. The coverage function then found no annotated phosphosites,
emitted no rows, and PTEN appeared in the output as a clean zero, indistinguishable from
BRCA1's genuine zero. Nothing raised an error.

The repair (`repair_sequenceless_entries`) re-queries by gene prefix and accepts a
replacement **only if its sequence is an exact match to that assay's ProteinGym
`target_seq`** — annotation is never adopted on name resemblance, since a wrong-organism
ortholog would silently shift every position. Three entries were repaired: `PTEN_HUMAN`
→ P60484, `CCDB_ECOLI` → P62554, `RNC_ECOLI` → P0A7Y0. Eleven remain unresolvable
(accession-style viral/bacterial names) and are now reported explicitly in
`still_sequenceless` rather than absorbed as zeros; for the glycosylation track those
assays are covered by the sequence-motif route regardless.

PTEN turned out to be one of Track A's better contributors — 11 annotated phosphosites,
all mapped, 11 with DMS coverage, including the CK2/ROCK1 tail cluster S380/T382/T383/
S385. Repairing it moved the headline figures from n = 13,359 / cell 250 to
**n = 14,130 / cell 260**, and the phospho arm from 30 to **40** usable positions.

Two lessons, both already applied: a zero produced by a *lookup* and a zero produced by
*biology* must never render identically, and this is now the second time in this pass
that mechanically auditing a claim against source data overturned it (the first being the
fabricated Bause quote, §7.2). Both errors were caught by verification, not by review of
the prose.
